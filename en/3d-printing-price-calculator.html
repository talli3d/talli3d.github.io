Questions

How is the price worked out?

For one plate: filament cost (grams × price per kg ÷ 1000), plus machine time (hours × your hourly rate), plus extras. That is multiplied by the number of plates, then by your markup and the complexity factor, and the setup fee is added once. The result never goes below your minimum price and is rounded up to the step you choose.

What if the last plate is not full?

Type Pieces needed. The calculator works out how many plates you print, and the last plate is charged only for the pieces on it. For example, 25 pieces at 8 per plate is 3 full plates plus 1 piece, charged as 3.125 plates.

Where do the grams and print time come from?

From your slicer (Bambu Studio, OrcaSlicer, PrusaSlicer, Cura and others) after you slice one plate. Enter the numbers for one plate and the calculator multiplies them.

What's the difference between single-nozzle and multi-nozzle printers?

On a single-nozzle printer (for example one with an AMS), every colour change purges filament and takes time, so the calculator adds grams and minutes for each change. A multi-nozzle or tool-changer printer keeps each colour in its own nozzle, so a change only costs a short tool swap, unless you use more colours than nozzles. If you drop a sliced 3MF, these are already in the slicer's numbers.

What machine rate should I use?

Enough to cover electricity, nozzles and other parts, maintenance and the printer's own cost over its life. The numbers filled in are only examples, so change them to your own.

Is it free? Is my data saved?

It's free, with no sign-up. Your rates and last job are kept only in this browser, on your device. Nothing you type is sent anywhere.
